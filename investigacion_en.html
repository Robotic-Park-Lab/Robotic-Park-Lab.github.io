Research

Lines, proposals and projects

The lab's research focuses on the control and navigation of multi-agent robotic systems: formations of drones and mobile robots, event-based control, cooperative cyber-physical systems and mixed reality. Everything relies on Robotic Park, an experimental platform integrated in ROS 2 with real and virtual aerial and ground robots. This page gathers the research lines, the open BSc and MSc thesis proposals and the projects the lab has taken part in. Publications are listed under Publications.

Research lines

Multi-robot formation control

Distance-based formations, virtual surface constraints, Lagrange multipliers and formation reconfiguration or morphing in multi-agent systems.

Event-based control

Event-based sampling and communication strategies with adaptive thresholds, applied to formation control and aerial platforms (Crazyflie).

Cooperative cyber-physical systems

Efficient design and distributed control of heterogeneous cyber-physical systems, with attention to communication and computation constraints.

Resilience and security

Resilient and secure control architectures and algorithms against faults and attacks in cooperative cyber-physical systems.

Multi-agent mixed reality

Real and virtual agents combined (digital twins in Gazebo and Webots) to scale swarm experiments beyond the available physical space.

Identification and hybrid control

Relay identification, PID autotuning and new paradigms for hybrid identification and control in high-performance systems.

Aerial robotics

Calibration, attitude and position control, gimbal tests and distributed formations with Crazyflie 2.X quadrotors.

Field robotics

Autonomous navigation of mobile and collaborative robots for transport tasks in Mediterranean greenhouses.

Cooperative navigation of multi-robot formations through morphing strategies

Supervisors: Francisco José Mañas Álvarez (fjmanas@dia.uned.es), Raquel Dormido Canto (raquel@dia.uned.es) and María Guinaldo Losada (mguinaldo@dia.uned.es)

The work will consist of the study, development and implementation of morphing strategies for the cooperative navigation of multi-robot systems. The goal is to let a robot formation dynamically change its geometry to adapt to environmental constraints, such as obstacles, narrow corridors or mission changes, while keeping the group's cohesion and connectivity. Distributed control and coordination algorithms based on geometric representations of the formation will be developed, evaluated first in simulation (ROS 2/Gazebo) and, optionally, on real robotic platforms. The work covers concepts of cooperative control, multi-agent robotics and trajectory planning.

Dynamic model identification and controller tuning for robots using Factor Graphs and GTSAM

Supervisors: Francisco José Mañas Álvarez (fjmanas@dia.uned.es), Raquel Dormido Canto (raquel@dia.uned.es) and María Guinaldo Losada (mguinaldo@dia.uned.es)

The work will consist of researching and applying optimisation techniques based on Factor Graphs to identify dynamic models of aerial robots and automatically tune their controllers. The GTSAM library (Georgia Tech Smoothing and Mapping) will be used to formulate parameter estimation problems from experimental flight data. From the identified models, the design and tuning of attitude and position controllers will be studied, evaluating their performance in simulation and, optionally, on real aerial platforms. The work provides knowledge of probabilistic estimation, nonlinear optimisation, system identification and advanced control of unmanned aerial vehicles.

Robot prototyping for hybrid multi-agent systems

Supervisors: Francisco José Mañas Álvarez (fjmanas@dia.uned.es), Raquel Dormido Canto (raquel@dia.uned.es) and María Guinaldo Losada (mguinaldo@dia.uned.es)

Dynamic robot simulation is essential to set up many robotics applications and helps decide whether a robotic system suits an environment and its characteristics. The work will consist of the complete modelling and development of a robot in ROS 2 with an open-source simulator, building an interface that lets the same code run on the simulated and the real robot. The robot will include its standard sensors and the devices needed to integrate it with other positioning systems. An interface that can be integrated into the Robotic Park experimental platform will be developed, aimed at hybrid multi-agent systems.

Development of the digital shadow of a mobile robot with ROS 2

Supervisors: Francisco José Mañas Álvarez (fjmanas@dia.uned.es), Raquel Dormido Canto (raquel@dia.uned.es) and María Guinaldo Losada (mguinaldo@dia.uned.es)

A digital shadow is a virtual replica of a physical system that can be used to simulate, analyse and optimise its performance in real time in different scenarios, and is characterised by one-way, real-time communication from the real robot. This project proposes to design the digital shadow of a mobile robot in different scenarios using ROS 2: create the virtual model of the robot, integrate its sensors and actuators with the virtual model and simulate typical scenarios for this kind of robot, such as model training, fault detection, dynamic controller tuning or autonomous navigation.
